## I. The Core Concept: Why Phrasal Verbs Matter at C1
At B2 level, we tend to rely on formal, Latin-based verbs (e.g., *investigate*, *eliminate*, *postpone*). While grammatically correct, this makes emails and spoken English sound robotic or overly academic. 

At C1 level, native speakers in the tech and business world use phrasal verbs to sound natural, direct, and collaborative. The goal is not to replace formal verbs entirely, but to know when to use the natural alternative.

### The B2 vs. C1 Shift
- **B2 (Stiff/Formal):** "We need to *investigate* the issue and *eliminate* the bug."
- **C1 (Natural/Professional):** "We need to *look into* the issue and *iron out* the bug."

## II. Essential Phrasal Verbs for Problem Solving & Innovation
These are critical for daily stand-ups, debugging, and proposing new AI solutions.

### Coming up with solutions
- **Come up with:** To think of or produce an idea, plan, or solution.
  - *Context:* "We need to **come up with** a better architecture for the new LLM integration."
- **Figure out:** To understand or solve something after thinking about it.
  - *Context:* "I'm trying to **figure out** why the API latency spiked yesterday."
- **Drill down into:** To examine something in great detail.
  - *Context:* "Let's **drill down into** the user retention metrics for Q3."

### Fixing and refining
- **Iron out:** To solve minor problems or difficulties.
  - *Context:* "The code works, but we need to **iron out** a few edge cases before deployment."
- **Patch up:** To make a quick, temporary fix to a system or relationship.
  - *Context:* "We **patched up** the security vulnerability, but a permanent refactor is needed."

## III. Project Management & Execution
Used heavily when discussing timelines, deliverables, and remote team coordination.

### Starting and finishing
- **Roll out:** To introduce or launch a new product, feature, or system gradually.
  - *Context:* "We are going to **roll out** the new dashboard to beta users next Monday."
- **Phase out:** To gradually stop using something.
  - *Context:* "We need to **phase out** the legacy SQL database by the end of the year."
- **Follow through (on):** To complete a task or promise successfully.
  - *Context:* "It's not enough to propose the automation; we must **follow through on** the implementation."
- **Carry out:** To perform or complete a task (more formal, but very common).
  - *Context:* "The QA team will **carry out** the regression tests tonight."

## IV. Communication & Remote Meetings
Essential for sounding polite but direct in Slack, Zoom, and emails.

### Discussing ideas
- **Bring up:** To mention a topic or start to talk about something.
  - *Context:* "I'd like to **bring up** the issue of scope creep during the next sync."
- **Run (something) by (someone):** To tell someone an idea to get their opinion or approval.
  - *Context:* "I have a new prompt engineering strategy. Can I **run it by** you quickly?"
- **Touch base:** To make brief contact with someone to update or get an update.
  - *Context:* "Let's **touch base** on Friday to see how the migration is going."
- **Circle back:** To return to a topic or person later.
  - *Context:* "I don't have the exact figures right now. Let me **circle back** to you after lunch."

## V. Grammar Pitfalls: Separable vs. Inseparable
A common C1 mistake is placing the object in the wrong position.

### Separable Phrasal Verbs
The object can go *between* the verb and the particle. If the object is a pronoun (it, them, me), it **MUST** go in the middle.
- *Correct:* "We need to **roll out** the update." OR "We need to **roll** the update **out**."
- *Correct (Pronoun):* "We need to **roll** it **out**."
- *Incorrect:* "We need to **roll out** it."

### Inseparable Phrasal Verbs
The object must always come *after* the particle.
- *Correct:* "We need to **look into** the issue."
- *Incorrect:* "We need to **look** the issue **into**."
